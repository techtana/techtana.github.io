I once advised an engineering intern to begin her internship by drafting the final presentation on her experience. I value clarity, and reducing guesswork is a foundation for building trust. Beyond discussing her internship goals and career vision, putting those ideas into writing—or even into a draft presentation—creates a tangible anchor. It establishes accountability for the intern, myself, and the team. This simple exercise not only guides the opportunities we can offer but also helps the intern build a personal narrative around the growth they want to pursue.

I learned this approach during my own internship, inspired by my professor’s advice: “Know why you started, know why you stayed, and know why you quit.” Without clarity and reminders, it’s easy to drift or make choices based on temporary emotions. By reflecting on purpose and vision, decisions become grounded in logic and long-term fit. For interns, the experience is not only an extended interview for the company—it’s also their chance to evaluate whether the role, the work, and even the city align with their career vision.

The draft presentation serves as more than just a future deliverable. It reminds us, as mentors and teammates, that we share responsibility in creating meaningful opportunities for interns to prove themselves. At the same time, it encourages interns to show initiative, demonstrating the values we prize most on our team.
